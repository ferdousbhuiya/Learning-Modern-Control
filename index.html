Learn how gain K changes the final value and time constant τ changes response speed.

G(s)=K/(τs+1)

What to observe: at t=τ the response reaches about 63.2% of its final value. A smaller τ gives a faster response.

DESIGN LAB
Root-Locus / Gain Explorer
For G(s)=1/[s(s+2)], vary K and see where the closed-loop poles move.
s²+2s+K=0


MATLAB practice
Goal: understand that changing gain moves closed-loop poles. Pole position predicts damping, oscillation, and stability before you simulate the time response.









STATE-SPACE LAB
Eigenvalue & State Motion
Set a real part σ and imaginary part ω for a conjugate eigenvalue pair λ=σ±jω.


MATLAB practice
Goal: if σ<0, modes decay; σ=0 gives sustained oscillation; σ>0 gives growing motion. ω controls oscillation frequency.









PID LAB
PID Tuning Playground
Control a simple second-order plant and watch how P, I, and D change the response.






MATLAB practice
Goal: P reacts to current error, I removes persistent error, and D adds damping by reacting to error rate. Tune them while watching overshoot and settling.

THEORY & MATH DOCS
State-Space & Modern Control Reference
State-Space Form
ẋ=Ax+Bu
y=Cx+Du
A captures internal dynamics, B input influence, C output mapping, and D direct feedthrough.
Controllability
𝒞=[B AB … Aⁿ⁻¹B]
Full rank means the input can influence every state direction.
Observability
𝒪=[C; CA; …; CAⁿ⁻¹]
Full rank means internal states can be reconstructed from outputs over time.
Lyapunov Equation
AᵀP+PA=-Q
For Hurwitz A and Q>0, the positive definite solution P gives a quadratic stability certificate.
LQR Cost
J=∫(xᵀQx+uᵀRu)dt
Q weights state error; R weights control effort.
Discrete-Time Stability
x[k+1]=A_dx[k]+B_du[k]
Stable discrete poles lie strictly inside the unit circle.
